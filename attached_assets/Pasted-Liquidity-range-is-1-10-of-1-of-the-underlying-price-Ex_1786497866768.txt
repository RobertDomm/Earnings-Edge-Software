How to Identify Liquid Options

One of the easiest ways to improve your trading results is to avoid illiquid options.

An option may look attractive on paper, but if you cannot enter and exit efficiently, the bid/ask spread becomes an invisible commission that reduces your profits on every trade.

The goal is to trade stocks where market makers compete for your order, helping keep spreads tight.

Rule #1: Weekly Options Only

Requirement

✅ The stock must have weekly options.

Why?

Weekly options attract more traders, creating higher volume and tighter bid/ask spreads.

Monthly-only options generally have less activity and wider spreads, making them more expensive to trade.

Rule #2: Avoid 5-Cent Markets

Requirement

The option quotes should not move exclusively in $0.05 increments.

Look for quotes with penny increments, such as:

✅ $0.31
✅ $0.44
✅ $0.57
✅ $0.83

Avoid option chains where every quote looks like:

❌ $0.30
❌ $0.35
❌ $0.40
❌ $0.45

Why?

When market makers quote only in 5-cent increments, it usually indicates lower liquidity.

Penny pricing generally means:





More competition



Better fills



Lower trading costs

Rule #3: Test the Short-Term Option Chain

I typically evaluate the 11 DTE option chain.

Specifically, I examine options trading between approximately $0.30 and $0.60.

Why?

This is where Earnings Edge typically enters positions. I want to know how liquid the options are where I will actually be trading—not six months out.

Rule #4: Maximum Bid/Ask Spread

Stock Price Maximum Spread: 

$0–$100 $0.10 

$100–$250 $0.30 

$250–$500 $0.40 

$500–$1,000 $0.50

If the spread exceeds these limits, I remove the stock from consideration.

Why These Numbers?

Higher-priced stocks naturally have wider option spreads.

A $0.30 spread on a $250 stock is proportionally similar to a $0.10 spread on a $50 stock.

The goal is not to eliminate spreads. The goal is to avoid paying unnecessary transaction costs.

Summary

Before adding any stock to the Earnings Edge watchlist, ask four questions:

✅ Does it have weekly options?

✅ Are the options quoted in penny increments rather than only 5-cent increments?

✅ Does the 11 DTE option chain show good liquidity around the $0.30–$0.60 options?

✅ Does the bid/ask spread meet the maximum spread requirements?

If the answer to any of these questions is no, I simply move on to the next stock.
